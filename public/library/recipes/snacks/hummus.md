A small sauce or dessert component, adapted for cube molds and shopping in Oum El Bouaghi. Cook the complete recipe first, then freeze in molds and assemble with other frozen components.

**Assembly:** vegetable and grain cubes, as a warm sauce. **Cooker:** pressure cooker.

The stated number of servings divides the batch for nutrition. It does not predict the number of mold cavities; weigh and label each serving when packing.

## Ingredients (15 recipe servings; cube yield measured after cooking)
| Ingredient | Amount | Prep |
|---|---|---|
| Dried chickpeas (hommos) | 300 g | soaked overnight in plenty of water |
| Bicarbonate of soda (bicarbonate) | ½ tsp | for cooking the chickpeas |
| Olive oil (zit zitoun) | 40 g (3 tbsp) | |
| Garlic (thoum) | 3 cloves (15 g) | thinly sliced |
| Tahini (tahina) | 150 g | stirred well in the jar |
| Lemons (qares / citron) | 2 (~90 ml juice) | juiced |
| Cumin (kammoun) | ½ tsp | |
| Salt | 1 tsp | |
| Chickpea cooking water, cold | ~150 ml | |
| Sweet paprika (paprika doux) | a pinch per puck | optional, on top |

**Peanut-ginger variant:** swap 60 g of the tahini for 60 g peanut butter, fry 1 tsp grated ginger (*skinjbir*) with the garlic, add ½ tsp mild curry powder, and blend in a handful of coriander (*kosbor*).

## Local supplies and substitutions

- Buy dried legumes at the épicerie; soak overnight in the fridge in hot weather, discard that water and cook until fully tender. Keep legumes and liquid below the pressure cooker half-full mark. Older beans may need longer; acidic tomato sauce is added after they soften.
- Seasoning alternatives: for 1 tbsp curry doux, use 1 tsp each ground coriander, cumin and turmeric; use sweet paprika separately where listed.
- Épicerie alternatives: tahina can be made by finely grinding lightly toasted hulled jeljlane; thin with a little of the recipe’s oil and weigh the paste needed.
- The calculation covers the main ingredient list. Alternatives change nutrition and cost; figures are ingredient estimates, not a guarantee for every local brand. There is no grocery budget cap.

## Method

**Pressure cooker:** use the manufacturer’s minimum thin-liquid volume and fill limits (at most half full for beans, grains and foaming food). Times start at full pressure. On a stovetop cocotte, sauté with the lid off on the burner; an electric sauté button is not required. Add thick dairy/flour sauces after pressure cooking where possible, then reduce uncovered.

1. **Chickpeas:** drain the soaked chickpeas and put them in the pressure cooker with the bicarbonate and water to cover by 5 cm. **High pressure 25 min**, natural release 10 min. They should crush to a paste between your fingers; if not, 5 min more. Scoop out **200 ml of the cooking water**, put it in the fridge to go cold, and drain the rest.
2. **Garlic:** small pan, low heat: olive oil and sliced garlic for **2–3 min**, until soft and just turning pale gold, not brown. Take off the heat.
3. **Blend** the tahini, lemon juice, cumin, salt and 100 ml of the cold cooking water for **1 min**, until pale and fluffy.
4. Add the warm chickpeas, the garlic and all its oil, and blend **3–5 min**, scraping down twice, until very smooth. Add more cold cooking water a spoon at a time if it's stiff. It should be a little softer than you want to eat it; it firms up as it cools.
5. Taste: more salt or lemon if it needs it.

## Freeze

1. Weigh the cooked batch (without the pot) and divide by **15**. That weight is one recipe serving. Distribute meat, sauce and vegetables evenly. Use **125 ml food-safe cube molds**, leaving the maker's expansion space. A serving may occupy more than one cavity; bag all its cubes together, including any smaller final cube. Do not assume grams equal millilitres.
2. Portion promptly into shallow molds. Refrigerate or freeze within **2 hours** of cooking, or **1 hour above 32 °C**; do not wait for a whole pot to cool. Freeze in a single layer at **−18 °C** until solid. If the freezer has no capacity, chill promptly at **4 °C or below** and freeze as soon as capacity is available.
3. Unmold and bag by component or serving. Label the dish, date and **number of cubes per recipe serving**. Vacuum-seal only once solid. Use within one month for this system. See [the cube packing guide](../../docs/packing.md).

## Reheat

**Microwave from frozen:** place the labeled serving of cubes in a microwave-safe bowl. Add 15–30 ml water to rice, pasta or thick sauce, and cover loosely. At **800 W**, start with **3 min**, break up and stir, then heat in **1 min bursts**, stirring between each, until the centre of every component reaches **74 °C**. Rest **1 min** and check again. A mixed bowl or several cubes takes longer; use temperature, not a fixed total time. Add fragile cream sauces in shorter bursts once loosened. No prior defrosting or fresh garnish is required. Reheat only what you will eat; do not refreeze reheated food.
