## Local supplies and substitutions

- Seasoning alternatives: if powders are unavailable, replace 1 tsp garlic powder with 10 g finely crushed garlic and 1 tsp onion powder with 40 g finely grated onion, fully softened and dried in the recipe’s cooking fat before mixing in; use plain dried thyme if oregano is unavailable; zaâtar here means the herb, not a salted sesame blend.
- Épicerie alternatives: choose a sauce soja suitable for your halal requirements; if unavailable, replace each 15 ml with 15 ml halal stock plus a small pinch of salt and simmer, accepting a less fermented flavour; white sugar replaces cassonade gram for gram in this cooked dish.
- The calculation covers the main ingredient list. Alternatives change nutrition and cost; figures are ingredient estimates, not a guarantee for every local brand. There is no grocery budget cap.

## Method

1. **Prep the wings:** cut off the tips and split each wing at the joint into a drumette and a flat. Pat them **very dry** with paper towels (dry skin is what goes crisp). Split them into two bowls, about 1.8 kg each.
2. **Bowl A:** toss with the oil, lemon zest, garlic powder, onion powder, paprika, pepper, oregano and salt. Keep the lemon juice aside.
3. **Bowl B:** mix the garlic, coriander, cumin, curry powder, oil, cider vinegar, 1 tsp soy and salt, and toss the wings in it until coated.
4. Cover both and **marinate in the fridge 1 h** (up to overnight, which suits cook weekend: do it the evening before).
5. **Oven to 220 °C.** Line 3 large baking trays with baking paper (a rack on top of each is even better). Lay the wings out in a **single layer**, skin side up, not touching, keeping the flavours on separate trays.
6. **Bake 40–45 min**, turning them and swapping the trays round at 25 min, until deep golden and crisp, the juices run clear, and the thickest drumette reads **74 °C** at the bone.
7. While they bake, make the **glaze**, small saucepan, medium heat: brown sugar, white vinegar, 2 tbsp soy and ketchup. Bring to a simmer, stir in the cornflour water and bubble **1–2 min**, until it coats a spoon.
8. When the wings are done, squeeze the lemon juice over the A wings. Tip the B wings into a bowl, toss them in the glaze, spread them back on their tray and **bake 5 min more**, until sticky.


**For cubes:** after cooking, remove all bones and cartilage, check carefully for fragments, and chop the edible meat and skin. Keep the cooked glaze and tray juices; loosen with a little hot water and coat the chicken. Pack each flavour into separate molds. The microwave version will be tender, with no crisp-skin claim.

## Freeze

1. Weigh the finished batch and divide into **12 equal recipe servings**, distributing all ingredients evenly. Use **250 ml food-safe cube molds**. One serving can occupy several cavities; bag the complete set together and label its cube count. Leave the mold’s expansion space.
2. Refrigerate or freeze shallow portions within **2 hours** of cooking/mixing, or **1 hour above 32 °C**. Freeze in one layer at **−18 °C**; use the freezer’s actual rated capacity. If it is full, refrigerate promptly at **4 °C or below** and freeze as soon as capacity allows.
3. Once solid, unmold into labeled zip bags; vacuum-seal only frozen cubes. Use within one month. See [cube packing](../../docs/packing.md).

## Reheat

**Microwave from frozen:** place the labeled serving of cubes in a microwave-safe bowl and cover loosely. Add 15–30 ml water if the mixture is thick or dry. At 800 W start with 1 min for a small dessert/side cube, or 3 min for a meal serving, then break up and stir. Continue in 30–60 s bursts until the centre reaches **74 °C**. Rest 1 min and check again. Several cubes take longer; temperature determines readiness. Assemble with compatible already frozen cubes. The component is ready to assemble directly from frozen. Do not refreeze reheated food.
