A cooked soup component, adapted for cube molds and shopping in Oum El Bouaghi. Cook the complete recipe first, then freeze in molds and assemble with other frozen components.

**Assembly:** rice or mash cubes; add a chicken cube if more protein is needed. **Cooker:** pressure cooker + stovetop.

The stated number of servings divides the batch for nutrition. It does not predict the number of mold cavities; weigh and label each serving when packing.

## Local supplies and substitutions

- At the boucherie, ask for the named cut, trimmed and weighed without bone; mince should be hachée devant moi, about 10% fat. Braising cuts need the long cooking method; they are not a direct quick-fry steak substitute.
- Dairy: yaourt nature must be unsweetened; brassé describes texture and does not guarantee Greek-yogurt protein.
- Lentilles corail dissolve into the sauce. If only brown/green adas are available, cook them separately until soft before adding, then blend or mash if a smooth texture is needed; the same dry weight will have a different cooking time.
- Ask for orge perlé, hulled/pearled barley. Dchicha is cracked barley: it is a possible same-dry-weight variant, but thickens and cooks faster; follow its texture and add water gradually.
- Seasoning alternatives: for 1 tsp garam masala, mix ½ tsp ground coriander, ¼ tsp cumin, ⅛ tsp cinnamon and ⅛ tsp black pepper; optional cardamom/clove is only a pinch.
- The calculation covers the main ingredient list. Alternatives change nutrition and cost; figures are ingredient estimates, not a guarantee for every local brand. There is no grocery budget cap.

## Method

**Pressure cooker:** use the manufacturer’s minimum thin-liquid volume and fill limits (at most half full for beans, grains and foaming food). Times start at full pressure. On a stovetop cocotte, sauté with the lid off on the burner; an electric sauté button is not required. Add thick dairy/flour sauces after pressure cooking where possible, then reduce uncovered.


1. **Pressure cooker on sauté (high):** the oil, smen and onions for **8–10 min**, until lightly golden. Add the garlic, ginger and whole spices for 2 min, with a splash of water if it catches.
2. Add the beef and stir **5 min**, until it changes colour. Add the ground coriander, cumin, paprika, pepper, nutmeg, nigella and salt for 30 s, then the tomatoes, yogurt and **1.2 L water**. Scrape the bottom clean.
3. **Lid on, high pressure 45 min**, then natural release 10 min. The beef should break apart when pressed with a spoon; if not, 10 min more. (Pot instead: simmer covered 2–2 h 30 min.)
4. **Meanwhile, 8 L pot, medium:** the drained pulses, turmeric and **1.8 L water**. Bring to the boil, skim, then lid half on and simmer **45–50 min**, stirring every 10 min, until everything is soft and mushy (the split peas take longest). Add a little water if it gets too thick to stir.
5. Blend the pulses until smooth, with a stick blender in the pot or in the blender in batches (fill it only halfway and vent the lid as the manufacturer permits; never seal a hot mixture in a closed blender), and put them back in the 8 L pot.
6. Lift the beef out of the pressure cooker, fishing out the bay leaves, cinnamon stick and cardamom pods as you go. **Shred it** in a bowl with a potato masher or 2 forks into long strands. Tip the beef and all the cooking liquid into the pulses.
7. **Cook it down:** add the oats, and on low heat stir and mash against the side of the pot for **25–30 min**. It thickens and turns stringy and glossy (the "laced" look). **Stir often and scrape the bottom; it catches easily.** If it gets thicker than a thick porridge, add boiling water, 250 ml at a time. Stir in the garam masala and coriander and taste for salt.
8. **Leave it a little looser than you'd serve it:** it sets much thicker in the freezer.


**Prepare for cubes:** For molding, cut cooked meat, meatballs, sausage and vegetables to at most 1–2 cm. Stir solids and sauce together before dividing so every serving gets its share. Add only enough hot water to make a dry mixture spoonable; keep cooking juices.

## Freeze

1. Weigh the cooked batch (without the pot) and divide by **10**. That weight is one recipe serving. Distribute meat, sauce and vegetables evenly. Use **250 ml food-safe cube molds**, leaving the maker's expansion space. A serving may occupy more than one cavity; bag all its cubes together, including any smaller final cube. Do not assume grams equal millilitres.
2. Portion promptly into shallow molds. Refrigerate or freeze within **2 hours** of cooking, or **1 hour above 32 °C**; do not wait for a whole pot to cool. For this rice dish, aim to get the shallow portions into the fridge or freezer within **1 hour**. Freeze in a single layer at **−18 °C** until solid. If the freezer has no capacity, chill promptly at **4 °C or below** and freeze as soon as capacity is available.
3. Unmold and bag by component or serving. Label the dish, date and **number of cubes per recipe serving**. Vacuum-seal only once solid. Use within one month for this system. See [the cube packing guide](../../docs/packing.md).

## Reheat

**Microwave from frozen:** place the labeled serving of cubes in a microwave-safe bowl. Add 15–30 ml water to rice, pasta or thick sauce, and cover loosely. At **800 W**, start with **3 min**, break up and stir, then heat in **1 min bursts**, stirring between each, until the centre of every component reaches **74 °C**. Rest **1 min** and check again. A mixed bowl or several cubes takes longer; use temperature, not a fixed total time. Add fragile cream sauces in shorter bursts once loosened. No prior defrosting or fresh garnish is required. Reheat only what you will eat; do not refreeze reheated food.
