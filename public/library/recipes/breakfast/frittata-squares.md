A fully cooked breakfast component, adapted for cube molds and shopping in Oum El Bouaghi. Cook the complete recipe first, then freeze in molds and assemble with other frozen components.

**Assembly:** other breakfast cubes as needed. **Cooker:** stovetop + oven.

The stated number of servings divides the batch for nutrition. It does not predict the number of mold cavities; weigh and label each serving when packing.

## Local supplies and substitutions

- Dairy: fromage rouge means gouda/edam-style block cheese for grating, not spreadable triangles; if feta is unavailable, use the same weight of firm, well-drained plain jben, crumble it and season to taste; do not use dried klila as a direct substitute.
- Produce: washed chopped blettes can replace spinach by trimmed weight; cook chopped stalks with the onions before adding the leaves.
- Seasoning alternatives: use plain dried thyme if oregano is unavailable; zaâtar here means the herb, not a salted sesame blend.
- The calculation covers the main ingredient list. Alternatives change nutrition and cost; figures are ingredient estimates, not a guarantee for every local brand. There is no grocery budget cap.

## Method

1. **Potatoes:** slices in a bowl with 3 tbsp water, covered, **microwave 6–7 min** until just tender. Drain.
2. **Big frying pan, medium heat:** olive oil and onion with a pinch of salt for **8 min** until soft. Add the garlic, peppers and zucchini for **8–10 min**, until the pan is dry and the zucchini is lightly browned. Stir in the spinach for **2 min**. Cool 10 min.
3. **Oven 180 °C.** Oil a **30 × 40 cm** deep oven tray and line the base with parchment. Lay the potato slices over the base, overlapping.
4. Whisk the eggs, milk, oregano, salt and pepper. Stir in the veg, olives, feta, two-thirds of the fromage rouge and the parsley. Pour over the potatoes and scatter the rest of the cheese on top.
5. Bake **35–40 min**, until puffed, golden and set in the middle (a knife comes out clean). Don't overbake or it goes rubbery.
6. Cool **15 min**, then cut into **15 squares** (3 × 5).


**Prepare for cubes:** For an egg or oat bake, crumble or cut the cooked soft mixture into small pieces and press into the cube mold while warm. Porridge can be spooned in directly. Freeze only after the egg or batter is fully cooked; a breakfast cube should be moist, not a crisp pastry.

## Freeze

1. Weigh the cooked batch (without the pot) and divide by **15**. That weight is one recipe serving. Distribute meat, sauce and vegetables evenly. Use **250 ml food-safe cube molds**, leaving the maker's expansion space. A serving may occupy more than one cavity; bag all its cubes together, including any smaller final cube. Do not assume grams equal millilitres.
2. Portion promptly into shallow molds. Refrigerate or freeze within **2 hours** of cooking, or **1 hour above 32 °C**; do not wait for a whole pot to cool. Freeze in a single layer at **−18 °C** until solid. If the freezer has no capacity, chill promptly at **4 °C or below** and freeze as soon as capacity is available.
3. Unmold and bag by component or serving. Label the dish, date and **number of cubes per recipe serving**. Vacuum-seal only once solid. Use within one month for this system. See [the cube packing guide](../../docs/packing.md).

## Reheat

**Microwave from frozen:** place the labeled serving of cubes in a microwave-safe bowl. Add 15–30 ml water to rice, pasta or thick sauce, and cover loosely. At **800 W**, start with **3 min**, break up and stir, then heat in **1 min bursts**, stirring between each, until the centre of every component reaches **74 °C**. Rest **1 min** and check again. A mixed bowl or several cubes takes longer; use temperature, not a fixed total time. Add fragile cream sauces in shorter bursts once loosened. No prior defrosting or fresh garnish is required. Reheat only what you will eat; do not refreeze reheated food.
