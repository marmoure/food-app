A fully cooked protein component, adapted for cube molds and shopping in Oum El Bouaghi. Cook the complete recipe first, then freeze in molds and assemble with other frozen components.

**Assembly:** rice, pasta or couscous cubes + vegetable cubes. **Cooker:** stovetop.

The stated number of servings divides the batch for nutrition. It does not predict the number of mold cavities; weigh and label each serving when packing.

## Local supplies and substitutions

- At the boucherie, ask for the named cut, trimmed and weighed without bone; mince should be hachée devant moi, about 10% fat. Braising cuts need the long cooking method; they are not a direct quick-fry steak substitute.
- Produce: qarnoun weights mean trimmed hearts, fresh in season or plain frozen; buy extra whole artichokes for trimming losses.
- The calculation covers the main ingredient list. Alternatives change nutrition and cost; figures are ingredient estimates, not a guarantee for every local brand. There is no grocery budget cap.

## Method

1. **Rice:** boil the rice in salted water for **12 min**, drain and cool on a plate.
2. **Mince:** in a big bowl, mix the mince with the garlic, half the parsley, egg whites, cinnamon, pepper and salt until sticky. Split into two halves. Roll one half into **14 meatballs** (~35 g). Mix the cooled rice into the other half for the stuffing.
3. Press a ball of stuffing (~38 g) into the dip of each artichoke heart, doming it slightly.
4. **Wide heavy pot, medium:** oil, smen and grated onion for **6–8 min** until soft. Add the **800 ml hot water**, the rest of the parsley, and a pinch of salt; bring to a simmer.
5. Lower in the stuffed hearts (meat side up, snug) and tuck the meatballs around them. **Lid on, simmer gently 30 min**, until the meat is cooked through and the hearts are tender when a knife goes in.
6. Lid off for **8–10 min** to reduce the sauce a little. Stir in the lemon juice off the heat and taste for salt.


**Prepare for cubes:** For molding, cut cooked meat, meatballs, sausage and vegetables to at most 1–2 cm. Stir solids and sauce together before dividing so every serving gets its share. Add only enough hot water to make a dry mixture spoonable; keep cooking juices.

## Freeze

1. Weigh the cooked batch (without the pot) and divide by **7**. That weight is one recipe serving. Distribute meat, sauce and vegetables evenly. Use **250 ml food-safe cube molds**, leaving the maker's expansion space. A serving may occupy more than one cavity; bag all its cubes together, including any smaller final cube. Do not assume grams equal millilitres.
2. Portion promptly into shallow molds. Refrigerate or freeze within **2 hours** of cooking, or **1 hour above 32 °C**; do not wait for a whole pot to cool. For this rice dish, aim to get the shallow portions into the fridge or freezer within **1 hour**. Freeze in a single layer at **−18 °C** until solid. If the freezer has no capacity, chill promptly at **4 °C or below** and freeze as soon as capacity is available.
3. Unmold and bag by component or serving. Label the dish, date and **number of cubes per recipe serving**. Vacuum-seal only once solid. Use within one month for this system. See [the cube packing guide](../../docs/packing.md).

## Reheat

**Microwave from frozen:** place the labeled serving of cubes in a microwave-safe bowl. Add 15–30 ml water to rice, pasta or thick sauce, and cover loosely. At **800 W**, start with **3 min**, break up and stir, then heat in **1 min bursts**, stirring between each, until the centre of every component reaches **74 °C**. Rest **1 min** and check again. A mixed bowl or several cubes takes longer; use temperature, not a fixed total time. Add fragile cream sauces in shorter bursts once loosened. No prior defrosting or fresh garnish is required. Reheat only what you will eat; do not refreeze reheated food.
